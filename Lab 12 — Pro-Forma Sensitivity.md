**Company:** Johnson & Johnson (NYSE: JNJ)

## D — My answer to the central question

I selected JNJ because it has two complementary healthcare businesses, Innovative Medicine and MedTech, substantial cash generation, and a clear forecasting question: can growth from newer medicines and MedTech offset STELARA sales lost to biosimilar competition? My supported conclusion is **watch/defer**, not buy or sell. JNJ is a high-quality company, but replacement-product growth, legal uncertainty, and inconsistent valuation outputs leave an insufficiently reliable margin of safety.

## R — Full analysis route

### 1. Target selection

JNJ is suitable because it is diversified across prescription medicines and medical technology, yet it faces a company-specific near-term risk: STELARA erosion. I began with a favorable view of its cash generation and product portfolio, then tested whether the cash-flow outlook supported that view.

### 2. Company and evidence

JNJ earns revenue through Innovative Medicine, which sells prescription medicines, and MedTech, which sells medical devices and related technologies used by hospitals and clinicians. Innovative Medicine generated **$60.4bn** in FY2025 sales.

My primary evidence is JNJ's [FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/200406/000020040626000016/jnj-20251228.htm), especially Item 1 for the business, Item 7/MD&A for product and growth trends, and the financial statements for historical inputs. I also used the FY2025 results release, Q2 2026 results release, and historical price data. FY2025 revenue was **$94,193m**; reported growth was 6.0%.

### 3. Pro-forma and accounting checks

I did not extend 6.0% growth mechanically because STELARA erosion was a material headwind and acquisitions contributed to reported growth. My forecast revenue-growth path is **5.0%, 4.0%, 4.0%, 3.5%, and 3.0%**. Other principal assumptions are 68.0% gross margin, SG&A at 37.0% of gross profit, R&D at 15.5% of revenue, capex at 5.0% of revenue, and an 18.0% tax rate.

Revenue drives gross profit, operating expenses, working capital, capex, FCFE, and cash. Each forecast year passed the accounting check, **assets − liabilities − equity = 0.0** within display rounding. Cash remained above the $10.0bn minimum-cash floor.

### 4. Valuation

My September 10, 2026 FCFF DCF is an enterprise-value model. Starting FCFF was **$21,325.1m**; explicit FCFF growth was 6%, 5%, 4%, 3%, and 3%; WACC was **5.88%**; and terminal growth was **3.0%**. I added $19,709m cash, subtracted $47,933m debt, and divided by 2,429.4m diluted shares. The result was **$320.59/share**.

The peer P/E cross-check uses Abbott and Merck as qualified, not identical, peers. The September 10, 2026 reference range was **$219.25–$306.47/share**, with a $262.86 median. I do not average the methods: the DCF depends on explicit cash-flow and discount-rate assumptions, while the peer reference depends on peer comparability and reported EPS. JNJ's reported EPS also has a talc-reserve-reversal comparability limitation.

The saved market price was **$267.08/share on September 9, 2026**. Holding starting FCFF, WACC, terminal growth, cash, debt, and shares fixed, reverse DCF implied a uniform -3.8032 percentage-point shift to explicit FCFF growth: approximately 2.20%, 1.20%, 0.20%, -0.80%, and -0.80%. This is one price-consistent input set, not proof of mispricing.

### 5. Sensitivity and drivers

The FCFE base-case result is **$138.28/share**. Changing every annual revenue-growth rate by -/+1 percentage point produced **$133.62–$143.06/share**, a $9.44 span. Changing gross margin from 67% to 69% produced a $8.59 span. Revenue growth is the larger tested driver only across these chosen ranges; its annual changes compound for five years, unlike the one-point margin range.

The causal chain is:

`revenue growth → revenue and gross profit/EBIT → working capital and capex → FCFE → equity value/share`

The sensitivity is conditional, not a probability forecast. WACC and terminal growth also matter because much of DCF value comes after the explicit forecast period.

### 6. Interpretation

I would become more constructive if quarterly product-level evidence shows DARZALEX, CARVYKTI, TREMFYA, CAPLYTA, and MedTech growth consistently offset STELARA erosion; margins and normalized cash flow hold; and litigation obligations become clearer. I would remain cautious if replacement products disappoint, biosimilar/competitive pressure or price pressure rises, litigation costs recur, or the required return rises.

My next research priority is a product-level revenue bridge: quarterly STELARA erosion versus growth in the replacement portfolio and MedTech. The question that most makes me reconsider the analysis is whether reported FY2025 EPS is recurring given the talc-reserve reversal; that strengthens the need to describe the P/E result as a reported-EPS reference rather than a standalone recurring-earnings valuation.
